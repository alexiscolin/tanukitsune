# v0.2 spec: anyone reviews their own account

Anyone who holds a WaniKani key reviews their own account on the public deployment. Ships when every
acceptance criterion below passes.

Read [`v0.1.md`](v0.1.md) for what the loop already does, `../framing.md` for why the architecture is
shaped this way, and `../backlog.md` for what was considered and rejected.

## What ships

The start screen takes a WaniKani key. With one, the reader reviews their own account: their queue,
their subjects, their history, and their answers sent back to WaniKani. With none, the seeded demo
deck, exactly as today. One public URL serves both, which is what v0.1 already promised.

The key is the identity. There is no sign-up, no password, no session to recover: a key names one
account at WaniKani and grants what that account holds, so the product asks for nothing else.

## Out of scope, explicitly

No password, no email, no account recovery. No second device carrying a reader's choices across:
the key lives in one browser and so does what the reader chose beside it. No sharing, no groups, no
per-reader corpus. No admin surface, and no support path to act on somebody's data on their behalf.

## Where the key lives

**The key travels in a cookie and is never written to the database.** It is set on the response that
accepts it, `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Strict`, and it is read again on every request that needs
it. The page renders on the server, so the key has to be readable at render time, and a cookie is the
one store that is.

The consequences follow from that. The key never reaches a log, an error message, a URL, or a client
bundle. It is sent to WaniKani and to nothing else. Clearing the cookie is signing out, and the
screen offers it.

**The reader identity is WaniKani's.** Accepting a key calls the source's `user` endpoint once, which
is what tells a working key from a typo. v0.1 reads that answer for the level a subscription grants and
for whether it is active; the account's own identifier is on the same answer and is parsed here for the
first time, since nothing needed it while one account was the whole product.

## Data model

`review_event` gains two columns, both written when the row is appended:

- `reader_id`, the WaniKani account the answer belongs to. Every read filters on it: the flush, the
  backup, and the statistics that come later. The rows written before this version carry the author's
  identifier, set by the migration, since they are the author's answers.
- `submits`, whether the answer may be sent back to WaniKani. True by default, false where the reader
  turned sending off before answering. It is a fact about the answer rather than a setting the flush
  reads later, because the table is append-only and a setting changed afterwards would rewrite what
  the reader chose at the time.

Nothing else is stored about a reader. There is no reader table: with no password and no second
device there is nothing to keep in one, and the key is already in the browser.

## The start screen

It gains three things, and keeps everything it has.

**A field that takes a key**, with the two states a reader meets: a key that works, which reports the
account name and level and starts the tunnel, and a key that does not, which says so and keeps the
field. A key is never shown back in full once accepted.

**A switch for sending answers to WaniKani**, on by default. Off means the answers are kept here and
the account is left alone, which is what the end-to-end suite already does through
`TANUKITSUNE_UPSTREAM_WRITE`. The deployment switch stays and wins: a deployment that sends nothing
sends nothing whatever a reader chose. The choice is held on the device beside the key, for the reason
the key is: there is no reader table to put it in, and a reader is their browser.

**A control that deletes the reader's history**, every row carrying their identifier, at once and
without a second channel to confirm through, since the product knows nothing about them but that
identifier. It says how many rows it removed.

## What the flush becomes

The flush runs for one reader, on their own request, with their key: it claims the rows carrying their
identifier and `submits`, sends them to their account and marks them. It never reaches for a row of
another reader, and a reader with no key has nothing to flush.

The subscription is read from the account the key names, so what a reader is shown is what their own
subscription grants, which is the rule v0.1 already holds for one account.

## Acceptance criteria

- A key pasted on the start screen deals that account's queue, and a wrong key is refused with a
  message rather than an empty session
- No route reads a key from anywhere but the cookie, and no response, log or bundle carries one
- A reader's answers are written with their identifier, and no read returns another reader's rows
- The rows written before this version carry the author's identifier after the migration
- An answer given with sending off is never submitted, whatever the switch says afterwards
- A deployment with no upstream write configured submits nothing, whatever a reader chose
- A flush sends only the rows of the reader whose key made the request
- No level beyond what the reader's own subscription grants is shown
- Deleting a history removes every row of that reader and no row of another, and says how many
- Signing out clears the cookie, and the next visit deals the demo deck
- The demo still runs with no key, and no route on it reads a key
- `pnpm verify` passes

## Verification

The end-to-end suite drives two readers against the fake source, which already answers as an account
nobody owns, and asserts that neither sees the other's rows. The cookie's attributes are asserted on
the response that sets it. The migration is tested against rows written before it.

A unit test holds the append path to writing both new columns, and the read paths to filtering on the
identifier, since a filter forgotten in one query is the whole of this feature failing quietly.
